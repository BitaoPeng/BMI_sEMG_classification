Executive Summary
To manage primary dysmenorrhea and associated cognitive/emotional symptoms, this project
proposes a non-invasive, hierarchical closed-loop BMI. With Reinforcement Learning (RL) for
adaptive Transcranial Alternating Current Stimulation (tACS) modulation and TinyML-enabled
fNIRS for deep-brain activity inference, the system offers personalized, drug-free pain manage-
ment. The hierarchical control strategy prioritizes pain alleviation while maintaining cognitive
productivity, and this wearable device transfers menstrual health management from passive
tracking to active and intelligent intervention via an edge SoC.

Introduction
Primary Dysmenorrhea (PD) is a significant global health concern which affects approximately
80% of women at some stage between menarche and menopause [1]. For 40% of these individ-
uals, they are even experiencing a complex of systemic symptoms, such as depression, reduced
concentration, and severe mood swings triggered by hormonal fluctuations [1,2]. These recurring
episodes lead to noticeable absenteeism from academic and professional activities, consequently
impacting women’s social well-being and overall quality of life [2]. Despite this burden, existing
interventions are suboptimal: non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) frequently have
gastrointestinal adverse effects, and hormonal contraceptives are unsuitable for adolescents due
to their potential interference with developmental growth.

Brain-Machine Interfaces (BMIs) offer a new way to assist in this context. Neuroimaging has
identified objective biomarkers for pain within the Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC) and Or-
bitofrontal Cortex (OFC) [3,4]; thus, the evaluation of pain has moved from subjective self-report
to quantified neural metrics. Women with PD exhibit abnormal functional connectivity within
the ACC [5].
Crucially, a closed-loop architecture is employed instead of manual control for several reasons:
first, it relieves the user’s cognitive burden of evaluating pain themselves when in distress;
second, it guarantees precise ”demand-based” stimulus dosing to prevent underdosing or over-
dosing due to users misjudging their pain levels. Furthermore, a closed-loop system enables
predictive intervention and preemptive suppression of pain signals before they reach personal-
ized, debilitating thresholds. Although these studies were based on invasive electrodes, recent
computational advancements demonstrate functional Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (fNIRS) can
reliably infer deep-brain activity (e.g., in the ACC) from cortical surface signals [6].
This project proposes a hierarchical closed-loop BMI utilizing non-invasive fNIRS and RL. fNIRS
is chosen over EEG due to its better motion tolerance, and practical convenience, as it eliminates
the need for applying conductive gels to female users with long hair. The ”hierarchical” approach
is twofold: temporally, it uses a dual-mode detection technique that monitors constant baseline
discomfort alongside acute uterine cramps [4]; functionally, the RL-based controller prioritizes
pain mitigation over emotional stabilization. Furthermore, AI deployment enables cross-subject
learning, hence personalization, and autonomous adaptation of the signal processing algorithm
over time due to signal quality degradation and neural plasticity.
By integrating predictive
algorithms and a lightweight, portable form factor to ensure long-term user compliance, this
personalized system offers a safe, drug-free solution for women to stay active and productive
throughout their menstrual cycles.

Methodology
The proposed system is designed as a low-power, real-time closed-loop BMI using embedded
TinyML techniques.
3.1
Signal Acquisition and Conditioning
A 16-channel fNIRS analog front-end (AFE) acquires hemodynamic signals from the prefrontal
cortex (PFC). To mitigate motion artifacts caused by muscle contractions during acute cramps,
a convolutional neural network (CNN)-based denoising algorithm is employed for real-time signal
preprocessing.
3.2
Cross-Modal Deep-Brain Inference
Due to the limited penetration depth of fNIRS, direct measurement of ACC activity is infeasible.
Instead, a pre-trained support vector regression (SVR) model infers ACC activation by analyzing
high-dimensional functional connectivity patterns extracted from surface PFC signals [6]. This
inference model is deployed using TinyML techniques, enabling efficient execution within the
memory and computational constraints of edge AI hardware.

3.3
Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning Control
A RL agent adaptively controls the tACS stimulator (e.g., α-band, < 2 mA) by tuning stim-
ulation frequency and amplitude [7], and it incorporates a predictive gradient analysis of the
inferred ACC signal, which initiates preemptive tACS modulation when the rate of pain signal
exceeds a personalized threshold.
• State Space: Inferred ACC pain biomarkers and direct PFC cognitive workload indi-
cators.
• Action Space: Real-time updates of tACS parameter sets.
• Reward Function: Primary optimization focuses on minimizing pain-related neural
activity. Once pain is suppressed below a predefined threshold, secondary optimization
maximizes cognitive focus and emotional stability.
The pain alleviation controller operates in a dual-mode manner: a tonic mode provides baseline
compensation for persistent discomfort, while an acute mode delivers transient, higher-intensity
stimulation in response to sudden cramp-related pain spikes.
3.4
Evaluation Metrics
• Pain Relief Efficacy: Reduction in objective pain intensity inferred from ACC activity.
• Cognitive Resilience: Performance improvement in standardized cognitive tasks dur-
ing stimulation.
