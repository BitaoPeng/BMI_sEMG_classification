Design of a flexible Continuous Time 
Sigma-Delta Converter for Neural 
Recording  
Description 
The field of neurotechnology relies on high-fidelity, high electrode count,  neural recording 
to decode complex brain activity for prosthetic control and disease monitoring. To move 
beyond bulky, wired setups, next-generation brain-machine interfaces require ultra-low-
power, miniaturized sensing nodes capable of processing massive data streams in real-time. 
Integrating a Continuous-Time Delta-Sigma (CT-ΔΣ) ADC directly at the electrode site is 
critical to maintaining signal integrity while minimizing front-end noise. 
This thesis will research a digitally-programmable, flexible ADC architecture designed to 
interface with diverse neural probes. By placing the converter as close as possible to the 
signal source, we can perform early-stage quantization that enables adaptive weighting of 
individual electrode readings. This flexibility allows the system to prioritize high-quality 
neural "spikes" while suppressing noise, providing a pre-processed data stream optimized for 
a downstream AI inference engine.  
This thesis is in cooperation with ICsense.
